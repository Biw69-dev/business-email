# Business Email (English)

Write professional English emails — concise, direct, and culturally aware.
Two modes: **Casual** (for colleagues) and **Formal** (for clients, bosses, elders).
Built with Japanese business culture in mind, but works for any professional setting.

## Core Principles

1. **Be concise** — NO long pleasantries about weather or health. Open briefly,
   state the point, close quickly. Target: 4-8 sentences total (Formal: 6-10).
   Every sentence must carry information the recipient needs.
2. **Simple words only** — No fancy vocabulary. Write like you speak. "Ask"
   not "inquire." "Help" not "assistance." "Sorry" not "I sincerely apologize."
   "Check" not "verify." "Send" not "provide." "Need" not "require."
3. **Context before request** — State the reason first, then the ask. One
   sentence each is enough.
4. **Soft but direct** — Polite ≠ long. "Could you send me the file?" works
   better than "I was wondering if it might be possible for you to provide..."
5. **Honorifics matter** — Use "San": Tanaka-san, not Tanaka or Mr. Tanaka.
6. **Group harmony (和 / wa)** — Use "we" more than "I," avoid blaming anyone.

## Email Structure

```
[Subject] Short, clear — what the email is about

Dear [Name]-san,

[One sentence opening — skip if not needed]

[Context — why you're writing, 1-2 sentences]

[Main point or request — 1 sentence]

[One line closing, ending with a comma],

[Name]
```

Target: 4-8 sentences from "Dear" to your name.

## Casual Mode (Default)

For close colleagues, daily teammates, follow-ups.

### Casual Openings

| Situation | Opening |
|-----------|---------|
| First contact | "I'm [Name] from [Company]. [Why contacting in one line.]" |
| After meeting | "Thank you for meeting on [day]. Following up on [topic]." |
| General follow-up | "Following up on [topic] from [when]." |
| Long time no contact | "It's been a while. Reaching out about [topic]." |
| Quick request | No opening — go straight to the point after "Dear [Name]-san." |

### Casual Requests

| Level | Phrase |
|-------|--------|
| Standard | "Could you [action]?" |
| Softer | "Would it be possible to [action]?" |
| Gentle reminder | "Just checking in on [topic] — any update?" |
| Suggestions | "How about [option]?" / "One idea: [suggestion]." |

### Casual Closings

End with comma, name on next line.

| Situation | Closing |
|-----------|---------|
| Standard | "Thanks for your time," |
| After receiving help | "Thanks again for your help," |
| Expecting reply | "Looking forward to your reply," |
| Before meeting | "Look forward to seeing you on [date]," |
| Ongoing project | "Thanks for your support," |

### Casual Templates

**Apology:**
```
Dear [Name]-san,

I'm sorry about [issue]. [One sentence explaining what happened.]

We are [fixing it by doing X] and will [finish by when].

Sorry for the trouble. Let me know if there's anything else I can do,

[Name]
```

**Declining:**
```
Dear [Name]-san,

Thanks for asking about [topic].

We can't [do X] because [short reason]. However, [alternative if possible].

Sorry about that. Hope you understand,

[Name]
```

**Thank You:**
```
Dear [Name]-san,

Thank you for [what they did] on [date]. [One specific thing you appreciated.]

[Next step if any, one line.]

Thanks again,

[Name]
```

## 🔷 Formal Mode

Use **Formal Mode** when the recipient is:
- A client or business partner (not a daily colleague)
- A boss, manager, or senior / senpai
- Someone you've never emailed before
- Someone much older or higher-ranking
- The topic is serious (formal apology, contract issue, complaint)

### Formal Mode Rules

**Same as Casual PLUS:**

1. **Short greeting is OK** — One simple line: "Hope you've been well." / "Hope your week is going well." No weather, no long pleasantries.
2. **More polite requests** — Use "I would appreciate it if you could..." or "Would it be possible to...?" instead of "Could you...?"
3. **Serious apology = stronger language** — "I sincerely apologize for..." + explain cause + fix + prevent. Show accountability.
4. **Acknowledge their time** — Add one line: "I understand you are busy" before a request.
5. **6-10 sentences allowed** — More context is OK when the situation is complex.
6. **Close with appreciation** — "Thank you for your time," not just "Thanks,"

### Formal Openings

| Situation | Opening |
|-----------|---------|
| First contact | "My name is [Name] from [Company]. I'm reaching out regarding [topic]." |
| After meeting | "Thank you for meeting on [day]. I appreciated the chance to discuss [topic]." |
| General follow-up | "Hope you've been well. Following up on [topic] from [when]." |
| Long time no contact | "Hope you've been well. It's been a while — reaching out about [topic]." |
| Serious topic | "Thank you for your time. I'm writing about [topic]." |

### Formal Requests

| Level | Phrase |
|-------|--------|
| Standard polite | "Could I ask you to [action]?" |
| More polite | "I would appreciate it if you could [action]." |
| Gentle reminder | "When you have a moment, could I check on [topic]?" |
| Proposal | "I would like to suggest [option] — would this work for you?" |

### Formal Closings

| Situation | Closing |
|-----------|---------|
| Standard | "Thank you for your time," |
| After help received | "Thank you again for your help," |
| Expecting reply | "I look forward to hearing from you," |
| Before meeting | "I look forward to seeing you on [date]," |
| Serious apology | "I sincerely apologize for the trouble," |

### Formal Templates

**Apology:**
```
Dear [Name]-san,

Thank you for your patience.

I sincerely apologize for [issue].
[What happened — factual, no excuses.]
[Why it happened — root cause, one line if you know it.]

We have [already done X to fix it] and are [doing Y to prevent it happening again].

I understand this may have caused [impact — delay, inconvenience, etc.].
Please let me know if there is anything more we can do.

Thank you for your understanding,

[Name]
```

**Request (First Time):**
```
Dear [Name]-san,

My name is [Name] from [Company]. I'm reaching out regarding [topic].

[One sentence context — why this matters.]

I understand you are busy, but I would appreciate it if you could [specific request].

Thank you for your time,

[Name]
```

## DOs and DON'Ts

### DO ✓
- Use "San" (Tanaka-san, not Tanaka or Mr. Tanaka)
- Open with one short line — then get to the point
- State reason before request (one sentence each)
- Use plain, simple English (ask, help, check, send, need — not inquire, assist, verify, provide, require)
- Close with one short line ending with a comma, then your name on the next line
- Casual: 4-8 sentences. Formal: 6-10 sentences.

### DON'T ✗
- **DON'T** open with "I hope this message finds you well" or weather talk
- **DON'T** use "Hey" or "Hi [first name]" — too casual
- **DON'T** make demands ("Send me the file by Friday.")
- **DON'T** use humor, sarcasm, ALL CAPS, or excessive !!!
- **DON'T** criticise anyone directly
- **DON'T** use fancy words (utilise, facilitate, endeavour, herein, furthermore)
- **DON'T** write more than 2 sentences without a line break
- **DON'T** assume quick replies — Japanese take time to consider

## Quick Tone Check

Before sending, check:

1. Right number of sentences? (Casual: 4-8, Formal: 6-10)
2. Any fancy words? Replace with plain English.
3. Any "I hope this message finds you well"? Remove — but a short "Hope you've been well" is OK in formal mode.
4. Is the request clear in one sentence?
5. Did I use "San"?
6. Anything that sounds bossy or harsh?

## ⚠️ MANDATORY: Ask Before Writing

**You MUST ask the user these questions BEFORE producing any email.**
Do NOT guess or fill in blanks yourself. Use the `clarify` tool.

Ask all four questions at once:

1. **Formal or casual?**
   - Formal: client, boss, senpai, first contact, elder, serious topic
   - Casual: close colleague, daily teammate, follow-up after meeting
2. **Who is the recipient?**
   - Name (e.g. Tanaka-san? Suzuki-san?)
   - Role / relationship
3. **What is the purpose?**
   - Request something? Thank you? Follow-up? Apology? Introduction? Decline?
4. **What details should the email include?**
   - Specific dates, project names, context, action items, attachments, deadlines, etc.

Only after the user answers all four, pick the right mode and craft the email.
Produce the email in clean, ready-to-send format.
